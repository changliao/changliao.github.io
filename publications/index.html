2026

  1. JAMES
    An Unstructured Voronoi Mesh Framework for Large-Scale Hydrologic and Land Surface Models
    Chang Liao, Darren Engwirda, Donghui Xu, Tian Zhou, Zeli Tan, and 1 more author
    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2026
    Accepted
    Abs Bib

    Accurate representation of river networks and land surface heterogeneity is crucial for improving the performance of continental-to-global hydrologic and land surface models (LSMs). Traditional approaches rely on either coarse-resolution grids, which may oversimplify complex terrain and limit the accurate representation of key processes, or computationally demanding full-domain high-resolution meshes. This study introduces a novel framework for generating Hydrologic Refined Meshes (HRMs) that seamlessly integrates crucial land surface features, including river networks, watershed boundaries, and lake boundaries, into the mesh generation process within the Model for Prediction Across Scales (MPAS) architecture.

  2. J. Hydrol.
    Systematic Evaluation of Atmospheric Forcing, Surface Datasets, and Mesh Effects on Kilometer-Scale Land Surface and River Modeling
    Lingcheng Li, Gautam Bisht, Donghui Xu, Dalei Hao, Chang Liao, and 2 more authors
    Journal of Hydrology, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Earth system models are advancing toward kilometer-scale resolution to capture local climate impacts and extremes. High-resolution land and river modeling depends on multiple factors, including mesh, surface datasets, and atmospheric forcing. We evaluated five Energy Exascale Earth System Model land and river configurations over the Mid-Atlantic region using structured versus variable-resolution unstructured meshes, two surface datasets, and three atmospheric forcings.

  3. Vadose Zone J.
    Influence of Plateau, Slope, and Valley on Soil Hydrology During the Dry Season in a Central Amazon Old-Growth Forest
    Robinson Negron-Juarez, C. Wright, S. Ferreira, K. Hirai, M. Kobayashi, and 8 more authors
    Vadose Zone Journal, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Soil moisture regulates plant water supply and drought sensitivity in tropical forests, yet its vertical and topographic variation remains poorly characterized. We combined high-frequency time-domain reflectometry measurements from 5 to 100 cm across plateau, slope, and valley landforms at the Zona Florestal 2 research site north of Manaus, Central Amazonia, to quantify how soil moisture memory, timing of responses to rainfall, dry-down rates, and soil-water depletion vary across contrasting landforms.

  4. JAMES
    ELM-MOSART-DOC: A Large-Scale Riverine Dissolved Organic Carbon Model and Its Application Over the United States
    Lingcheng Li, Hong-Yi Li, G. Abeshu, X. Yang, J. Tang, and 3 more authors
    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Riverine dissolved organic carbon (DOC), primarily sourced from soil organic carbon (SOC), plays a crucial role in regional and global carbon cycles. We introduce ELM-MOSART-DOC, a DOC module integrated into the riverine component of the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM)—the Model for Scale Adaptive River Transport (MOSART), simulating DOC transport and transformation across headwater streams and river networks.

2025

  1. NHESS
    Disentangling Atmospheric, Hydrological, and Coupling Uncertainties in Compound Flood Modeling Within a Coupled Earth System Model
    Dongyu Feng, Zeli Tan, Darren Engwirda, J. D. Wolfe, Donghui Xu, and 7 more authors
    Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Compound riverine and coastal flooding is usually driven by complex interactions among meteorological, hydrological, and ocean extremes. We evaluate compound uncertainties arising from two-way land-river-ocean coupling in E3SM and track cascading meteorological and hydrological uncertainties through ensemble simulations over the Delaware River basin and estuary during Hurricane Irene (2011).

  2. ESSD
    Discrete Global Grid System-Based Flow Routing Datasets in the Amazon and Yukon Basins
    Chang Liao, Darren Engwirda, Matthew G. Cooper, M. Li, and Y. Fang
    Earth System Science Data, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Discrete Global Grid systems (DGGs) are emerging spatial data structures widely used to organize geospatial datasets across scales. This study pioneers the development of new flow routing datasets using Icosahedral Snyder Equal Area (ISEA) DGGs and a novel mesh-independent flow direction model for the tropical Amazon River Basin and the Arctic Yukon River Basin.

  3. ESSD
    Deriving a Transformation Rate Map of Dissolved Organic Carbon over the Contiguous U.S.
    Lingcheng Li, Hong-Yi Li, G. Abeshu, J. Tang, L. Ruby Leung, and 5 more authors
    Earth System Science Data, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    We propose a generic formula that directly connects SOC concentration with DOC concentration in headwater streams and derive a high-resolution DOC transformation rate map over the contiguous U.S. using machine learning and catchment-scale attributes.

  4. GMD
    Representing Lateral Groundwater Flow From Land to River in Earth System Models
    Chang Liao, L. Ruby Leung, Yilin Fang, Teklu Tesfa, and Robinson Negron-Juarez
    Geoscientific Model Development, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Lateral groundwater flow (LGF) is an important hydrologic process in controlling water table dynamics. In this study, we developed a hillslope-based lateral groundwater flow model based on Darcy’s equation and coupled it to the land component (ELM) and river component (MOSART) of the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM).

  5. ERL
    Changes in Soil Water Content and Lateral Flow Exert Large Effects on Soil Thermal Dynamics Across Alaskan Landscapes
    X. Liu, Qianlai Zhuang, Chang Liao, and M. T. Jorgenson
    Environmental Research Letters, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Both lateral surface and subsurface water flow affect soil moisture dynamics. Here we use a 3-D ecosystem model that considers both land surface and subsurface hydrologic processes to simulate soil moisture, which is then used to drive a 1-D vertical soil thermal model to simulate soil moisture effects on soil thermal dynamics in central Alaska.

  6. JAMES
    Evaluation of Flow Routing on the Unstructured Voronoi Meshes in Earth System Modeling
    Chang Liao, Donghui Xu, Matthew G. Cooper, Tian Zhou, Darren Engwirda, and 5 more authors
    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2025
    Abs Bib

    Flow routing is a fundamental process of Earth System Models’ river components. This study extends the river component of the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM) to unstructured Voronoi meshes and evaluates the model’s performance in simulating river discharge and water depth across Arctic, temperate, and tropical watersheds.

  7. New Phytol.
    Improving the Representation of Plant Water Stress and Water Use in Earth System Models
    J. S. Dukes, C. Xu, Chang Liao, K. A. Novick, R. P. Phillips, and 7 more authors
    New Phytologist, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Projections of terrestrial climate feedbacks from Earth System Models depend on simulated carbon and water dynamics of vegetation. Here, we discuss how models simulate plant hydraulic processes and identify opportunities to improve confidence in model outputs, focusing on vegetation physiology, hydrodynamics, and groundwater flow models.

    A Multi-Algorithm Approach for Modeling Coastal Wetland Ecogeomorphology
    Zeli Tan, L. Ruby Leung, Chang Liao, Luca Carniello, Jose F. Rodriguez, and 4 more authors
    Frontiers in Earth Science, 2024

  2. JAMES

    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2024

In this study, we present a novel multi-scale coupling framework within E3SM, integrating atmosphere and land with interactively coupled river and ocean models on variable-resolution meshes near coastlines.

  3. GMD
    Disentangling the Hydrological and Hydraulic Controls on Streamflow Variability in E3SM V2 – A Case Study in the Pantanal Region
    Donghui Xu, Gautam Bisht, Zeli Tan, Chang Liao, Tian Zhou, and 2 more authors
    Geoscientific Model Development, 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Streamflow variability plays a crucial role in shaping ecosystem dynamics. In this study, we performed experiments with the land and river components of E3SM over the Pantanal region to disentangle hydrological and hydraulic controls on streamflow variability and propose a two-step joint calibration procedure.
