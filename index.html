I am an Earth Scientist at the Institute of Atmospheric Physics (IAP), Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS) in Beijing, China. Prior to joining CAS in 2026, I worked as an Earth Scientist and Postdoctoral Research Associate at the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL). I received my Ph.D. in Earth, Atmospheric, and Planetary Sciences from Purdue University.

The overarching goal of my research is to establish flexible, operational, and scalable modeling frameworks to investigate and predict global water, carbon, and energy cycle dynamics under changing climates and extreme events.

Research Interests

  • Land Surface & Earth System Modeling: Kilometer-scale land surface simulations, coupled water-carbon dynamics, and soil hydrology.
  • Mesh-Independent Hydrology & River Routing: Unstructured Centroidal Voronoi Tessellations (MPAS) and hexagonal grid frameworks for multi-scale flow direction and river networks.
  • Compound Flooding & Hydro-Climate Extremes: Two-way coupled river-coastal hydrodynamic modeling and uncertainty quantification in extreme events.
  • High-Performance Computing (HPC): Developing scalable Earth system models and parallel scientific software in C++, Fortran, and Python.

selected publications

  1. JAMES
    An Unstructured Voronoi Mesh Framework for Large-Scale Hydrologic and Land Surface Models
    Chang Liao, Darren Engwirda, Donghui Xu, Tian Zhou, Zeli Tan, and 1 more author
    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2026
    Accepted
    Abs Bib

    Accurate representation of river networks and land surface heterogeneity is crucial for improving the performance of continental-to-global hydrologic and land surface models (LSMs). Traditional approaches rely on either coarse-resolution grids, which may oversimplify complex terrain and limit the accurate representation of key processes, or computationally demanding full-domain high-resolution meshes. This study introduces a novel framework for generating Hydrologic Refined Meshes (HRMs) that seamlessly integrates crucial land surface features, including river networks, watershed boundaries, and lake boundaries, into the mesh generation process within the Model for Prediction Across Scales (MPAS) architecture.

  2. ESSD
    Discrete Global Grid System-Based Flow Routing Datasets in the Amazon and Yukon Basins
    Chang Liao, Darren Engwirda, Matthew G. Cooper, M. Li, and Y. Fang
    Earth System Science Data, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Discrete Global Grid systems (DGGs) are emerging spatial data structures widely used to organize geospatial datasets across scales. This study pioneers the development of new flow routing datasets using Icosahedral Snyder Equal Area (ISEA) DGGs and a novel mesh-independent flow direction model for the tropical Amazon River Basin and the Arctic Yukon River Basin.

  3. GMD
    Representing Lateral Groundwater Flow From Land to River in Earth System Models
    Chang Liao, L. Ruby Leung, Yilin Fang, Teklu Tesfa, and Robinson Negron-Juarez
    Geoscientific Model Development, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Lateral groundwater flow (LGF) is an important hydrologic process in controlling water table dynamics. In this study, we developed a hillslope-based lateral groundwater flow model based on Darcy’s equation and coupled it to the land component (ELM) and river component (MOSART) of the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM).

  4. JAMES
    Evaluation of Flow Routing on the Unstructured Voronoi Meshes in Earth System Modeling
    Chang Liao, Donghui Xu, Matthew G. Cooper, Tian Zhou, Darren Engwirda, and 5 more authors
    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2025
    Abs Bib

    Flow routing is a fundamental process of Earth System Models’ river components. This study extends the river component of the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM) to unstructured Voronoi meshes and evaluates the model’s performance in simulating river discharge and water depth across Arctic, temperate, and tropical watersheds.

  5. JAMES
    Topological Relationship-Based Flow Direction Modeling: Mesh-Independent River Networks Representation
    Chang Liao, Tian Zhou, Donghui Xu, Matthew G. Cooper, Darren Engwirda, and 2 more authors
    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2023
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Accurately representing river networks in spatially distributed hydrologic models is sensitive to model resolution. We developed a topological relationship-based river network representation method using graph theory algorithms to simplify vector networks and reconstruct conceptual river networks across structured and unstructured meshes.

  6. JOSS
    PyFlowline: A Mesh-Independent River Network Generator for Hydrologic Models
    Chang Liao, Matthew G. Cooper, Donghui Xu, Tian Zhou, Gautam Bisht, and 1 more author
    Journal of Open Source Software, 2023
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF Code

    PyFlowline is an open-source Python package that generates mesh-independent river networks. With PyFlowline, hydrologic modelers can generate conceptual river networks at various spatial resolutions for both structured and unstructured computational meshes.

  7. JAMES
    Topological Relationship-Based Flow Direction Modeling: Stream Burning and Depression Filling
    Chang Liao, Tian Zhou, Donghui Xu, Zeli Tan, Gautam Bisht, and 4 more authors
    Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 2023
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    This follow-up study presents a novel DEM processing approach for flow direction modeling consisting of a topological relationship-based hybrid breaching-filling stream burning method and a modified depression removal method for rivers and hillslopes across arbitrary mesh systems.

  8. AWR
    Advances in Hexagon Mesh Based Flow Direction Modeling
    Chang Liao, Tian Zhou, Donghui Xu, Richard Barnes, Gautam Bisht, and 6 more authors
    Advances in Water Resources, 2022
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Watershed delineation and flow direction representation are the foundations of streamflow routing in hydrologic modeling. In this study, we explored approaches such as spatial resampling and hybrid breaching-filling stream burning techniques to improve watershed delineation using HexWatershed in the Columbia River basin.

  9. EMS
    Watershed Delineation on a Hexagonal Mesh Grid
    Chang Liao, Teklu Tesfa, Zhuoran Duan, and L. Ruby Leung
    Environmental Modelling & Software, 2020
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    We developed a watershed delineation model (HexWatershed) based on hexagonal grid spatial discretization to overcome the limitations of Cartesian grids, such as non-uniform adjacency, showing significant advantages for spatially distributed hydrologic simulations.
